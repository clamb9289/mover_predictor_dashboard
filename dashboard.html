How the Model Works — v2

Overview

Each morning before market open, the predictor runs a series of checks before selecting any stocks. It first checks whether the overall market is healthy enough to trade. If the market passes, it scores the full S&P Composite 1500 (~1,500 large/mid/small-cap stocks) on four weighted signals, then selects the top 3 most likely to move during the upcoming session. Stocks with upcoming earnings or excessive recent volatility are excluded entirely. (An overnight gap-down reranking boost also lives in this step — currently disabled, see below.)

Step 1 — Market Regime Check

Before any picks are made, the model checks whether SPY (S&P 500 ETF) is down more than 1.5% from its prior close. If it is, no trades are made that day. Fighting a down market is one of the fastest ways to lose money consistently — sitting out on bad days protects capital. (Recalibrated 2026-07-17 from an untested 0.5% — 438 real candidate outcomes across 44 trading days showed no cliff there; if anything, days that would've been skipped at 0.5% had a higher win rate. Raised to 1.5% as a still-conservative circuit breaker for genuinely extreme days rather than picking a precise number off a thin tail.)

Step 2 — Universe Filter

The full S&P Composite 1500 — the S&P 500 (large-cap) plus the S&P MidCap 400 and S&P SmallCap 600, about 1,500 tickers total — is narrowed down before scoring begins. Stocks must have an average daily volume of at least 1.5 million shares and a price between $5 and $600. This eliminates illiquid names that can spike randomly and are hard to exit cleanly, while still catching mid/small-cap movers that a large-cap-only universe would miss entirely.

Step 3 — Earnings Exclusion

Any stock with an earnings announcement within the next 3 days is automatically excluded. Earnings are a coin flip — a stock is just as likely to drop 10% as gain 10% on an earnings surprise. For a strategy targeting consistent 1–5% daily gains, earnings events are pure unnecessary risk.

Step 4 — Volatility Exclusion (added 2026-07-29)

Any candidate whose trailing 10-day Average True Range exceeds 4.0% of its price is excluded, regardless of how well it otherwise scores. Calibrated across 250 real trading days (714 real substitution-simulated trades, chronological train/validation split): the highest-volatility candidates lose noticeably more often than calmer ones without a corresponding gain in average return — a real cost to consistency with no offsetting upside, not just a correlation with rare big winners. Filtering them out and promoting the next-ranked candidate instead raised win rate from 71.7%/67.1% (train/validation) to 75.7%/80.1%, holding on both halves of the data, with average return essentially unchanged.

Scoring Signals

Accumulation Signal
60%
High volume WITHOUT a big price move. This is the signature of smart money quietly buying before a move. Score = volume ratio ÷ (1 + price move). A stock with 3x normal volume but a flat price scores very high.
Sector Momentum
20%
How well has the stock's sector performed over the last 5 trading days? Stocks in the top-performing sectors get a bonus. The sector tide lifts individual stocks — picking against a weak sector is an uphill battle.
News Sentiment
0%
Headlines from the last 48 hours are still fetched and shown on each pick for context, but as of the 2026-07-10 weekly Model Review, this signal carries zero scoring weight — it no longer influences which stocks get picked.
Relative Strength
20%
The stock's 5-day return vs its sector's return. A stock outperforming its own sector shows individual demand beyond market tailwinds.

Overnight Gap-Down Boost (added 2026-07-17, disabled 2026-08-19)

Currently disabled. When active, this was a flat score bonus applied to shortlisted candidates whose price had gapped down 5%+ overnight (prior close → today's open) — not a weighted signal like the four above, and calibrated across 250 trading days back when it shipped, isolating specifically the days it changed the top-3: the promoted pick beat the pick it replaced by roughly +1.4 percentage points of win rate on both a training and a held-out validation window. That calibration predated the swing-trading switch by a week and was never re-tested under real multi-day hold mechanics — re-run at 736-day scale on 2026-08-19 (the largest single-parameter test this project has run), disabling the boost beat the live baseline on both win rate and average return, on both splits. The deep-gap-down mean-reversion edge that justified promoting a candidate under the old single-day model doesn't carry the same weight once a position has 3 days to develop instead of 1. Threshold/mechanism left in the code, just zeroed out, in case future evidence says otherwise.

Trade Execution Rules

🕙
Buy right at the 9:30am ET open (changed 2026-07-31 from a 5-minute 9:35am buffer; retry window added 2026-08-19 after a real self-hosted-runner communication failure caused a full trading day's buy to silently not fire — now dispatches 4 times, 5 minutes apart (9:30/9:35/9:40/9:45am ET) every day, not just when something's wrong. Each dispatch only fills whatever slots are still genuinely empty, so on a healthy day the first one does everything and the other 3 are harmless no-ops; the redundancy only matters on a day like 2026-08-19's) — scoring itself now runs at 9:10am off the prior day's close (doesn't need market hours; as of 2026-08-20 this is the FULL candidate pipeline — accumulation shortlist, earnings/volatility filtering, news sentiment, sector momentum, composite score — not just raw price data). Only the live market regime check, a fresh news-sentiment re-check on the top 20 candidates, the live opening-gap boost, and the actual buy wait for the real open. Firing right at 9:30 rather than 5 minutes later gets broker-side resting stops placed on new positions as early as possible, minimizing the window they sit unprotected. The old buffer was never doing volatility protection on its own — the opening-move and opening-gap filters below handle that regardless of what minute the buy fires.
⛔
Opening-move filter — at buy time, a pick already down 5%+ from today's open is skipped rather than bought (still breaking down, not just normal early noise). Backtesting across our own picks showed win rates hold up fine on shallower drops and only turn bad past this depth (5%+ drops: 40% win rate), so the threshold sits there rather than shallower. A bounce-off-low override was also tested and dropped — it showed no consistent effect across any test.
📈
Opening-gap filter — a pick that's already gapped up 1%+ from the prior close its own signals were scored on is skipped. Traced from a real trade (NEM, 2026-07-14: scored off a $93.10 close, opened at $97.81, faded back to ~$94.80 by afternoon) and confirmed across 612 historical picks — gaps of 1%+ showed a -0.38% average return and 35% win rate vs. +0.47%/51% below that threshold. This catches a different failure mode than the opening-move filter above: a move that already happened before today's open, which none of the scoring signals (based on prior daily bars) could see.
⏱
Monitor every ~60 seconds, continuously (moved 2026-08-07 off the old cron-job.org-dispatched GitHub Actions job — which had tightened to a 2-minute schedule 2026-07-29 but was actually only landing every ~2 minutes at best, since real checkout/setup overhead kept colliding with its own dispatch queue — onto a native systemd timer running directly on the trading VM itself, with no dispatch layer in between. The timer ticks year-round; outside market hours each tick is a near-instant no-op, so this doesn't change anything about when real position checks happen, only how tight they are once the market's open. Cadence itself corrected once before that, 2026-07-24 — the old cron-job.org monitor job actually started at 10:00am, a real 25-minute blind window every morning after the buy where no position got checked at all; tightened to start shortly after the buy instead. As of 2026-07-31 the buy itself also moved to right at the 9:30am open — see the buy-timing rule above — with the very first check now running inline as part of that same buy trigger, not waiting for the next scheduled tick).
🟢
Staircase trailing stop — the floor steps up as the stock climbs. Hit +0.5% → floor locks at +0.375% (recalibrated 2026-07-16: simulated 309 real candidate trades across a chronological train/validation split, testing this tier against several trigger/floor combinations — this one gave a consistent +5pp win-rate improvement on both halves of the data over the prior +1%/+0.5% tier, without hurting average return; a lower 0.25% trigger or a 50%-of-trigger floor each captured only about half that benefit). Hit +1.25% → floor steps up to +0.9% (added 2026-07-24 after a real position peaked +2.11% but was still only protected to +0.375%, since the staircase previously had no step between 0.5% and 3%; tested against 382 train / 178 validation real candidate trades, this step raised the average exit for trades peaking in that gap from +0.44% to +0.51%/+0.49% while also improving overall win rate on both splits, with no cost to average return). Hit +2% → floor steps up to +1.5% (added 2026-08-04 after a real position peaked +2% but was still only protected to +0.9%, since a gap still remained between 1.25% and 3%; tested against 228 train / 105 validation real candidate trades under current swing-hold/broker-stop mechanics, this step raised the win rate for trades peaking in that gap from 83.3%/91.4% to 84.2%/92.4% and average return from +0.28%/+0.46% to +0.37%/+0.57%, train/val). Hit +3% → +3%. Hit +5% → +5%. Hit +7% → +7%. Hit +10% → +10%. Hit +15% → +15%. Beyond +15%, the floor trails dynamically 2% below the all-time intraday peak. If the stock falls back below the active floor at any check, it's sold to lock in that gain — the active floor is shown live on each pick's card.
🚀
No intraday upside cap — during the trading day, a rising position keeps climbing with no hard ceiling, protected only by its rising floor. At end of day, though, see the EOD profit-taking rule below — any gain still standing gets banked rather than left to run overnight.
🔴
Hard stop at -3% — position sold immediately to cap losses.
📆
Up to 3 trading days per position (swing trading, added 2026-07-24, cap reduced from 5 to 3 on 2026-07-29 — the original 5-day calibration never modeled capital recycling; a real 3-slot compounding simulation over 250 days found 3 days beats both 1-day and the old 5-day on compounded return) — a position that hasn't hit its stop-loss or floor keeps running instead of being forced closed same-day. A slot freed by an exit gets refilled with the next-ranked candidate within the same ~60-second tick, not held open until the next morning — except within 25 minutes of market close (added 2026-07-31 as a 10-minute cutoff, widened to 25 on 2026-08-19 — precisely matched to the EOD sell's new 3:35-3:55pm retry window below, since nothing can free a slot for EOD-sell reasons before that first 3:35pm attempt even exists — so a slot freed by an early sell attempt still can't be immediately re-bought), where a freed slot is deliberately left empty overnight rather than buying a new position with no real time left in the session to manage it.
🔔
End-of-day profit-taking around 3:35-3:55pm ET (added 2026-07-25 as a single 3:55pm dispatch; changed to a 3-attempt window 2026-08-19 — 3:35/3:45/3:55pm ET, 10 minutes apart — after a real self-hosted-runner failure showed a single dispatch has no safety net. Each attempt sells whatever's still open and profitable, so whichever one actually runs first typically does the real selling and the later ones are harmless no-ops — meaning the effective time is usually 3:35pm now, not fixed at 3:55pm. The 10-minute spacing (not 5) specifically allows for a hung attempt blocking the next one in queue for a while, same failure shape as 2026-08-19's incident. See the 3-day-hold rule above for the matching change to the no-rebuy-near-close guard, widened the same day so a slot freed by an early sell attempt still can't be immediately re-bought before end of day) — any position still up at end of day is sold immediately, banking the gain rather than carrying it overnight, regardless of how far above its floor it's sitting. Losing positions get NO special treatment here — they stay open under the stop-loss/staircase rules above, up to the 3-day cap, giving them a chance to recover. Evaluated per-trade in isolation this looked like a wash (higher win rate, lower variance, but a smaller average return per trade than holding regardless of sign) — but simulated through the real 3-slot/immediate-refill structure (capital freed sooner gets reinvested sooner), it won decisively: roughly 1250% vs 670% total return over the same 250-trading-day window, holding on both independent halves of that window too, not just the period as a whole.
🛡
Daily loss limit: 5% — any open positions are liquidated immediately and the bot pauses for the rest of the day if hit (found 2026-07-15: it used to only pause new trades, leaving already-open positions unmonitored for the rest of the day — fixed to liquidate first).
⛔
Total drawdown shutoff: 15% — any open positions are liquidated immediately, then the bot disables itself permanently until manually re-enabled.
📅
No trades on bearish market days — if SPY is down more than 1.5% from prior close, no positions are opened that day.
📊
Earnings blackout — stocks with earnings in the next 3 days are never picked regardless of their score.
〰️
Volatility exclusion (added 2026-07-29) — a candidate with trailing 10-day ATR above 4.0% of its price is skipped and the next-ranked candidate is promoted instead. Backtested across 714 real substitution-simulated trades over 250 days: raised win rate from 71.7%/67.1% (train/validation) to 75.7%/80.1%, with average return essentially unchanged — high-volatility picks lose more often without winning bigger on average to compensate.

What May Be Added Next

A weekly Model Review (see that tab) now tracks which signals actually correlate with outcomes and backtests alternate weight presets against real historical data, flagging a candidate change if one meaningfully outperforms — reviewed by hand, never applied automatically. A pre-market/after-hours price-gap scanner (see the Scanners tab) is also live, but track-only for now — it doesn't feed into live picks until enough data validates it. (An hourly intraday volume/momentum scanner ran the same way through July 2026 but was retired 2026-07-31 — no validated edge after three weeks of real data, plus a real operational cost from monopolizing the shared runner; see the Scanners tab.) A news-volume/LLM-sentiment correlation check is also being tracked (see the LLM sentiment data) but needs a few more weeks to accumulate before it's meaningful. Other potential future improvements: options flow as a sentiment signal. (Sector concentration limits and partial profit-taking were both tested against real historical data in 2026-07 and didn't hold up — not pursued further without new evidence.)
